# The Age of Digital Abundance — research and editorial basis

Prepared October 15, 2026. Unpublished article: `blog/age-of-digital-abundance.html`.

The editorial reference is `/Volumes/JackPack/c4573-ethos/ETHOS.md`, derived from the seventeen posts indexed when collected. Its core test is whether people gain useful capability they can retain. The article applies that test to personal information systems and software replacement, including the possibility that an AI provider becomes the next gatekeeper.

## Argument

The fragile assumption is compulsory dependence: that customers must rent a vendor's entire arrangement, and readers must pass through monetized interfaces to obtain information. User-controlled AI can make narrow alternatives economical. Publishing maintainable replacements can extend that option to others.

That is an economic hypothesis supported by examples and early substitution evidence. It does not establish that either industry is collapsing, that all subscriptions are exploitative, or that AI infrastructure investment is fairly valued. The article preserves the user's smoke metaphor while making its limits explicit.

## Boundaries and unresolved examples

The user has not supplied named SaaS replacement repositories. The draft therefore describes rebuilding and publishing as the project and strategy; it does not invent completed replacements, savings, customer counts or deployment results. Independent implementation is distinguished from redistributing proprietary source or impersonating a vendor.

The draft counts support, security, maintenance, error recovery, funding original reporting, information quality and new platform capture as real costs. Source links help verification but do not themselves pay a reporter. There is no prediction that everyone can immediately operate this workflow, no quantified timeline for industry destruction, and no claim that useful AI disproves excessive AI valuations.

Confidence should rise with persistent replacements, lower full operating costs, usable migration and payment reaching original information producers. It should fall if alternatives fail after initial demonstrations or simply transfer dependence to a new provider. Those tests are part of the article, not an appended disclaimer.
